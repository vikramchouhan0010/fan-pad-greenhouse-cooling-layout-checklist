# Fan and Pad Cooling System in a Greenhouse: Plan the Layout

## Check the climate before selecting equipment

Evaporative cooling depends on the difference between outside dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures. As outdoor air becomes more humid, the available cooling potential narrows. Wet-bulb temperature is a physical limit for evaporation alone, not a guaranteed temperature throughout the crop.

Ask the designer to state the outdoor design conditions and the expected conditions at crop level. Separate the hottest dry-weather case from the humid-season case. This makes two quotations comparable and exposes assumptions that a simple fan count would miss.

- Record the location, growing months and crop-stage targets.
- Ask which weather data and design day were used.
- Request a written explanation of conditions the proposed system cannot maintain.

## Draw the air route through the actual growing space

Show the wet-pad wall, exhaust wall, crop rows, bench heights and access doors on one plan. Air warms as it crosses the greenhouse, so the fan end can be warmer than the pad end. A reading beside the pad cannot represent the whole house.

Openings outside the designed inlet route can admit air that has bypassed the pad. Have the designer document the vent and door operating sequence in cooling mode, together with safe access and the ventilation mode used when evaporative cooling is off. Do not close or alter a greenhouse simply to copy a diagram.

- Mark insect screens and other airflow resistance in the design brief.
- Keep service access to both pad faces and fan assemblies visible on the drawing.
- Ask for measurement points near the pad, middle and fan end at crop height.

## Specify the pad water circuit and its service access

The water circuit distributes water over the pad and collects it below for recirculation. The quotation should identify the distribution arrangement, collection gutter, sump, pump, make-up water provision and maintenance access. Uniform wetting matters; increasing pump output is not a substitute for finding a blocked distributor or damaged pad.

Give the supplier a water-quality report and a description of the source. Ask for the selected pad manufacturer’s cleaning and water-management instructions. Keep chemical treatment out of the operating plan until its compatibility, handling procedure and disposal requirements are confirmed.

- Clarify which water-circuit parts are supplied and which are site work.
- Check that the sump arrangement accommodates drain-back after shutdown.
- Request safe isolation and access for cleaning without walking on the greenhouse frame.
